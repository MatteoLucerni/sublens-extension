Sublens

Subtitle language ? The language of the subtitles. "Auto" reads the active subtitle track from the player (Netflix or YouTube); pick a language to force it instead.
Translate to ? The language translations and dictionary definitions are shown in.
Enable on Netflix ? Turn the extension on or off for Netflix. When off, Netflix plays with its normal subtitles and the extension does nothing.
Enable on YouTube ? Turn the extension on or off for YouTube (watch pages). When off, YouTube plays with its normal captions and the extension does nothing.
Enable on Prime Video ? Turn the extension on or off for Prime Video (primevideo.com). When off, Prime Video plays with its normal subtitles and the extension does nothing.
Blur subtitles ? When enabled, subtitles are blurred so you read the dialogue without seeing the text at a glance. Hover over a subtitle to reveal it.
Back arrow rewinds subtitle ? When enabled, pressing the Left Arrow key jumps back to the start of the previous subtitle line and pauses at its end, instead of Netflix's default 10-second rewind.
Auto-pause on hover ? When enabled, hovering the mouse over a subtitle automatically pauses the video, and resumes it when you move the mouse away.
Remove blur when paused ? When enabled, pausing the video automatically reveals all blurred subtitles, so you can read them while paused.
Auto-pronounce on selection ? When enabled, the selected word or phrase is pronounced automatically in the subtitle language as soon as the translation popup opens. The speaker button in the popup is always available to play the pronunciation on demand, regardless of this setting.

How translation works

  • Click a word for its translation and dictionary definition.
  • Ctrl/Cmd + Click multiple words to combine them into one selection.
  • Click and drag across words to select a phrase.

Support Sublens

Sublens is free, open source, and has no ads and no tracking. It also runs inside three players it does not control: Netflix, YouTube and Prime Video reshape their caption markup without warning, and a change nobody announces can quietly break subtitles that worked yesterday.

Keeping it working means chasing those changes down one player at a time, in someone's spare time. Any of these three helps.

Buy me a coffee A one-off contribution on Ko-fi. No account and no subscription needed. Contribute on GitHub Send a pull request, or report a broken subtitle with the title and platform it happened on.
